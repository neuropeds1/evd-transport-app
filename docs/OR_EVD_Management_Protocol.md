# OR EVD Management Protocol

## Overview

Neurosurgical patients with External Ventricular Drains (EVDs) often require surgery or procedures in the operating room. This protocol provides a quick-reference checklist to guide anesthesia providers and surgical teams on how to handle EVDs during operative care.

## Purpose

The OR EVD Management Protocol serves as a safety checklist and reference for healthcare providers who may not handle EVDs frequently. It is grounded in perioperative guidelines from SNACC (Society for Neuroscience in Anesthesiology and Critical Care) and aims to ensure meticulous intraoperative EVD management, akin to ICU-level care in the OR.

## Protocol Components

### 1. Setup and Equipment

**Before Induction:**
- Ensure that the EVD is connected to a transducer cable (if ICP monitoring is planned in the OR)
- The anesthesia monitor must have an ICP channel ready
- Have a laser level or carpenter's level available in the OR to re-level the drain after positioning

**Key Reminder:** "Prepare ICP transducer and leveling device."

**Drainage Chamber Management:**
- If the patient is to remain on continuous drainage, ensure the drainage chamber is on the IV pole that will stay with the patient
- If the patient will be moved to the OR table, the EVD should move with them and remain level throughout the transfer

### 2. Labeling and Line Identification

**Critical Safety Measure:**
- Clearly label the EVD and its tubing with an identifying tag (often color-coded) to avoid confusion with other lines
- Use a prominent sticker or tape (e.g., "EVD – Do Not Infuse") on the drip chamber and stopcocks

**Rationale:** In the OR, multiple teams work around the patient. It's critical that everyone knows which line is the EVD to avoid:
- Accidentally injecting drugs into it
- Pulling it thinking it's something else
- Confusing it with other drainage or intravenous lines

### 3. Positioning Considerations

**After Surgical Positioning:**
- Reconfirm the level of the EVD relative to the external auditory meatus (tragus)
- This might need adjustment if:
  - The patient's head moved when transferring to the OR table
  - The surgical position is unusual (e.g., head turned or bed tilted)

**Protocol Reminder:** "After positioning, level EVD at tragus and secure at that height."

**Special Considerations:**
- In prone or lateral cases, this can be tricky
- The anesthesia team may need to coordinate with surgeons to find a spot for the EVD system that remains level with the head
- Failure to re-level could lead to unintended drainage or occlusion during the case

### 4. Intraoperative Monitoring

**Continuous ICP Monitoring:**
- Monitor ICP throughout the procedure if at all possible
- If the EVD is open to drain during the case, remember that the displayed ICP on the monitor is only valid when the drain is briefly closed

**Common Practice:**
- Intermittently clamp the EVD (for a minute or so) to obtain an ICP reading every hour, similar to ICU practice
- Document both:
  - EVD drainage setting and output hourly
  - ICP measurements hourly

**Documentation Template:**
Each hour note: "EVD at X cmH₂O, drained Y mL this hour, ICP = Z mmHg (measured with drain closed)."

**Importance:**
- Critical for trend tracking
- Makes handoffs safer (next provider knows what the pressures have been and how much CSF has come out)

### 5. Communicate with the Surgeon

**Required Communication:**
The anesthesia team must inform the surgeon promptly if any of the following EVD-related events occur:

**a) Sudden Cessation of CSF Drainage**
- When drainage was previously brisk
- Could indicate catheter occlusion or ventricle collapse

**b) Absence of CSF When Expected**
- May indicate the catheter is occluded
- Could signal ventricle collapse

**c) Unexpected Large CSF Output**
- If >20 mL drained in one hour
- May signal overdrainage and risk of:
  - Brain collapse
  - Hemorrhage

**d) Acute Change in CSF Appearance**
- New blood in the fluid
- Should be relayed immediately
- Could indicate intraoperative hemorrhage

**e) ICP Waveform Changes**
- If waveform dampens or disappears
- Could mean:
  - Catheter is dislodged
  - Transducer is off
- Team should troubleshoot at once

**Checklist Reminder:**
"If EVD stops draining or >20 mL drained or bloody return – inform surgeon and consider pausing procedure to address."

**Rationale:** 
- Keeps the surgical team in the loop
- EVD issues might necessitate surgical intervention
- Surgeon may need to adjust the catheter or address bleeding

### 6. Handoff Protocol

**During Intraoperative Handoffs:**
Ensure EVD details are passed on during:
- Handoffs between anesthesia providers
- Transition to the ICU team post-op

**Essential Information to Include:**
- Current drain status (clamped or open and at what level)
- Total CSF output during the case
- Last measured ICP
- Any problems encountered

**Handoff Template Example:**
"EVD open at 10 cmH₂O throughout case, ~50 mL total output over 4 hours, ICP ranged 5–15 mmHg clamped. No complications."

**Benefits:**
- Structured communication prevents errors
- Prevents EVD being accidentally left clamped
- Ensures high output goes recognized when patient returns to ICU
- Ensures continuity of care

## Implementation in Clinical Practice

### Real-Time Reminders

The app delivers these reminders in real time, such as:
- Pop-up: "Hour 1: record ICP and output now"
- Visual checklists to ensure adherence to best practices

### Expected Outcomes

By following this protocol, providers can:
- Avoid pitfalls such as uncontrolled ICP swings during surgery
- Prevent forgetting to resume drainage afterward
- Improve team communication
- Ensure everyone is aware of the drain's status
- Prepare team to act if anomalies occur

## Summary

The OR EVD Management Protocol functions as a cognitive aid and safety checklist for anesthesiologists and surgeons, ensuring that critical EVD management steps are not overlooked during the high-stress operative environment. By standardizing care through this protocol, we enhance patient safety and improve clinical outcomes in neurosurgical patients with EVDs undergoing operative procedures.
