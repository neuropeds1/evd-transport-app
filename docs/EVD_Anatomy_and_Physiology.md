# EVD Anatomy and Physiology Module

## Overview

This educational content is particularly useful for trainees (residents, nurses new to neuro ICU, etc.) who benefit from understanding the conceptual "why" behind EVD management.

## 1. Ventricular System Anatomy

### Overview

The ventricular system consists of interconnected spaces within the brain that contain cerebrospinal fluid (CSF).

### Components

**The Ventricles:**
- **Two lateral ventricles** (right and left)
- **Third ventricle**
- **Fourth ventricle**

### Interconnections

**Flow Pathway:**
1. **Lateral ventricles** drain through the **foramina of Monro** into the **third ventricle**
2. **Third ventricle** drains via the **aqueduct of Sylvius** into the **fourth ventricle**
3. **Fourth ventricle** passes CSF into the subarachnoid space via:
   - **Lateral apertures** (of Luschka)
   - **Median aperture** (of Magendie)

### Clinical Relevance

**EVD Placement:**
- EVD tip typically sits in the lateral ventricle near the foramen of Monro
- This helps users visualize why we level at the tragus
- The tragus approximates the height of the ventricular system
- Proper leveling ensures drainage occurs only when ICP exceeds the set threshold

## 2. CSF Production and Circulation

### Key Facts

**Production Rate:**
- ~20–25 mL/hour
- ~500 mL/day

**Total CSF Volume:**
- ~150 mL in the system

### Production Site

**Choroid Plexus:**
- Located in the ventricles
- Constantly produces CSF
- CSF circulates through the ventricles and around the brain and spinal cord

### Absorption

**Arachnoid Granulations:**
- CSF is reabsorbed into the bloodstream via arachnoid granulations
- Located primarily in the superior sagittal sinus
- Maintains equilibrium between production and absorption

### Clinical Significance

**Continuous Turnover:**
- The continual turnover means that an EVD can significantly alter intracranial dynamics
- By removing CSF faster than it's produced, overdrainage is possible if the system is set too low
- Must balance adequate drainage with prevention of overdrainage

## 3. Monro-Kellie Doctrine

### Fundamental Principle

**The Hypothesis:**
The skull is a rigid box containing three components:
1. **Brain tissue**
2. **Blood**
3. **CSF**

**Key Principle:**
An increase in any one component must be offset by a decrease in another, or else intracranial pressure will rise.

### Mechanism

**Compensatory Mechanisms:**
- When brain swelling occurs, CSF volume may decrease initially
- Cerebral blood volume may also adjust
- However, once compensatory mechanisms are exhausted, small volume increases lead to large ICP rises

### Clinical Application

**EVD Exploitation:**
- An EVD exploits this principle by removing CSF
- This reduces pressure when other components (brain tissue swelling or blood volume) increase
- Provides a safety valve when intracranial compliance is poor

**Limitations:**
- There are limits to compensation
- Once decompensated, aggressive management is required
- Timely drainage is necessary in acute hydrocephalus or traumatic brain injury

## 4. ICP Definitions and Norms

### Normal Values

**Adults:**
- Normal ICP: ~5–15 mmHg
- Sustained ICP > 20–22 mmHg is typically treated in neurocritical care

### Pathophysiology

**Elevated ICP:**
- Sustained elevation can lead to poor outcomes
- Reduction of cerebral perfusion pressure (CPP)
- CPP = Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) - ICP
- When CPP drops, cerebral blood flow may be compromised

### Clinical Signs

**Chronic Elevation:**
- Headache
- Vomiting
- Papilledema (chronic elevation)

**Acute/Pre-terminal:**
- Cushing's triad:
  - Hypertension
  - Bradycardia
  - Irregular respirations
- This represents imminent herniation - a neurosurgical emergency

### Clinical Correlation

Understanding these signs and normal values helps users:
- Correlate the numbers they see with clinical status
- Recognize when intervention is needed
- Understand the urgency of management

## 5. Drain Dynamics

### How EVD Works

**The Siphon Principle:**
An EVD is essentially a siphon that uses the pressure gradient:
- When ICP exceeds the set threshold (e.g., 10 cmH₂O), CSF will flow down into the chamber
- Flow continues until pressure drops to equilibrium
- If ICP is below the threshold, no CSF will drain

### Pressure Gradient

**The Math:**
- Pressure gradient drives CSF flow
- Set height = threshold pressure
- ICP > threshold → drainage occurs
- ICP < threshold → no drainage

### Clinical Scenarios

**Low Threshold Setting (e.g., 5 cmH₂O):**
- Will drain a lot of CSF
- Helps relieve hydrocephalus quickly
- Risks overdrainage
- May cause:
  - Brain collapse
  - Subdural hematoma
  - Ventricle collapse

**High Threshold Setting (e.g., 20 cmH₂O):**
- Uses EVD more as an ICP safety valve
- Won't drain unless pressure becomes significantly elevated
- Protects against extremes
- Appropriate when ICP is generally controlled

### Clinical Decision-Making

**Setting Selection:**
Different settings based on goals:
- **5 cmH₂O**: Aggressively drain (acute hydrocephalus)
- **10 cmH₂O**: Standard drainage (most common)
- **15-20 cmH₂O**: Safety valve only (SAH with unsecured aneurysm)

**Integration:**
This ties back to earlier content where clinicians choose different settings based on clinical goals.

## 6. Lumbar Drains vs EVD

### Comparison

**Similarities:**
- Both drain CSF
- Many principles overlap
- Used to control intracranial pressure

**Differences:**

| Feature | EVD | Lumbar Drain |
|---------|-----|--------------|
| **Placement** | Ventricular | Lumbar subarachnoid space |
| **Leveling** | External auditory meatus (tragus) | Spinal reference or phlebostatic axis |
| **Risk of Herniation** | Lower (ventricular drainage) | Higher (lumbar puncture physiology) |
| **Typical Use** | Acute hydrocephalus, ICP monitoring | Chronic normalization pressure hydrocephalus (NPH), certain surgical cases |

### Special Considerations

**Lumbar Drains:**
- May be leveled to spinal reference or phlebostatic axis
- Carry risk of different herniation syndromes if overdrained
- Can cause upward herniation in severe ICP elevation
- Not appropriate when ICP is significantly elevated

### Clinical Application

This helps broaden understanding and prepare users who might encounter both devices in clinical practice.
