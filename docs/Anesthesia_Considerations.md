# Anesthesia Considerations for EVD Management

## Overview

Patients with External Ventricular Drains (EVDs) present unique challenges to anesthesiologists. This document provides anesthesia-specific considerations for both ICU and OR settings, serving as a handy guide for trainees and practitioners.

## Key Clinical Areas

### 1. Pre-Induction Planning

**EVD Status Evaluation:**
- Evaluate the EVD status before anesthesia
- Determine if it's currently open and draining, or clamped
- Review patient's ICP trend

**Rationale:** 
Knowing this informs your induction strategy. For instance:
- If ICP is elevated but the EVD is clamped, induction and intubation could precipitate a dangerous spike
- One might request to keep the EVD open until the last moment
- Be prepared to manage a surge in ICP

**Essential Preparations:**
- Ensure a proper baseline neuro exam is done if possible
- Review all EVD data:
  - Recent outputs
  - ICP values
  - CSF appearance
- Have a plan with neurosurgery for how the EVD will be managed during induction
- Sometimes neurosurgery might temporarily open the EVD if safe, to allow drainage while the patient is manipulated

### 2. Intracranial Dynamics During Induction

**Avoid ICP-Increasing Factors:**
Avoid factors that dramatically increase ICP during induction:

**a) Smooth Induction**
- Perform smooth, relatively deep induction
- Prevent coughing or bucking on the endotracheal tube
- Coughing/bucking can sharply raise ICP

**b) Mitigate Pressor Response**
- Use lidocaine IV or intratracheal
- Consider opioids or other blunting agents
- Reduces the pressor response to intubation

**c) Blood Pressure Management**
- Avoid significant hypertension during laryngoscopy
- Treat blood pressure spikes promptly
- Hypertension can increase cerebral blood volume and ICP

**d) Ventilation Strategy**
- Some anesthesiologists will mildly hyperventilate the patient after induction
- Target PaCO₂ of ~30–35 mmHg if worried about ICP
- Lowering PaCO₂ causes cerebral vasoconstriction and lowers ICP
- Acts as a bridge until other measures are in place

**Important Note:** 
"Maintain PaCO₂ in low-normal range (e.g. 35 mmHg) if ICP is a concern, but avoid excessive hyperventilation which may reduce cerebral perfusion."

### 3. Positioning and Ventilation

**Head and Neck Positioning:**
- Ensure the head and neck remain neutral
- Keep elevated about 30° if possible during anesthesia
- Facilitates venous drainage
- Helps keep ICP down

**Special Considerations:**
- If surgical position mandates something different, work with surgeons
- If patient must be flat or in Trendelenburg:
  - Recognize the ICP implications
  - Prepare accordingly (maybe have the EVD open or increase drainage frequency)
- Avoid any neck taping or positioning that compresses the jugular veins

**Positive End-Expiratory Pressure (PEEP):**
- Be cautious with PEEP on the ventilator
- High PEEP can increase intrathoracic pressure
- Impedes cerebral venous outflow
- Raises ICP
- Use the lowest PEEP necessary for oxygenation

### 4. Intraoperative EVD Management

**Measurement Philosophy:**
- Cannot measure ICP continuously while the EVD is open to drain (with standard systems)
- Must decide on management strategy:

**Option A: Continuous Drainage**
- Keep EVD open continuously
- Maximizes drainage, minimizes ICP
- Trade-off: no continuous ICP readings

**Option B: Intermittent Drainage**
- Periodically clamp to check ICP
- More ICP monitoring data
- Appropriate if ICP is generally under control

**Clinical Decision:**
- If brain relaxation is a goal (e.g., in craniotomy for tumor), neurosurgeon may want it open
- If ICP is generally under control, intermittent drainage with periodic ICP checks might suffice

**Documentation:**
- Document all changes you make to the EVD
- Record time clamped/unclamped
- If CSF is draining freely, keep track of volume in the chamber
- Don't let it overfill

**Communication:**
- Communicate significant ICP readings or drain outputs to the surgeon periodically
- Especially important if outside expected ranges

### 5. Medication Considerations

**Anticoagulation Status:**
- Be mindful of anticoagulation status
- If the patient had an EVD placed, check that coagulation parameters are acceptable
- If on heparin or antiplatelets, there might be specific guidelines on when it's safe to proceed to surgery or remove the EVD

**Blood Pressure Management:**
- Avoid uncontrolled hypertension
- In some EVD patients (like SAH with unsecured aneurysm), a blood pressure spike could risk rebleeding
- One reason to sometimes set an EVD at a higher pressure is to avoid over-draining CSF
- This could drop ICP and cause a transmural pressure rise across an aneurysm

**SAH with Unsecured Aneurysm:**
- Special consideration required
- Anesthesiologists should be aware if the patient has an unsecured aneurysm
- In such cases, they often intentionally keep the EVD at a relatively high setting (15–20 cmH₂O)
- Prevents too rapid decompression

**Key Reminder:** 
"SAH with unsecured aneurysm – avoid excessive CSF drainage; maintain higher EVD threshold to reduce risk of rebleed."

### 6. Emergence and Postop

**Smooth Emergence:**
- Plan for a smooth emergence if the patient will wake up with the EVD in place
- Coughing on emergence can be just as stimulating as induction
- Consider deep extubation or lidocaine to the tube if appropriate
- Ensure sedation/analgesia are sufficient if the patient should not cough
- Post-op neuro patients often are kept intubated anyway

**EVD Management During Transfer:**
- Ensure the EVD is correctly managed going back to ICU
- Decide on clamped vs open during transfer
- Tie in the earlier transport checklist
- Ensure all information is handed off

**DVT Prophylaxis:**
- Hold DVT prophylaxis (e.g., heparin) around the time of EVD removal
- Many protocols stop heparin 4 hours before and after EVD removal
- Reduces bleeding risk
- Important to remember this in the OR or immediately post-op

## Clinical Pearls

### Why These Considerations Matter

**For Residents and Trainees:**
- EVDs are not everyday devices in many ORs
- Provides refresher on key points
- Example: "Oh yes, I must clamp the EVD when changing the bed position to avoid rapid drainage."

**For Experienced Providers:**
- Useful just-in-time reminder
- Reinforces evidence-based practice
- Cognitive aid during high-stress situations

### Educational Value

**Including Rationale:**
- Providing citations or rationale in the app (via tooltips or info icons) can further educate users
- Reinforces why each measure is recommended
- Improves understanding of the underlying physiology

### Real-World Applicability

This quick reference condenses nuanced knowledge that anesthesiology providers should have when managing an EVD patient. It acts as:
- A teaching tool for trainees
- A just-in-time reminder for experienced providers
- A safety net to prevent critical errors

## Summary

Anesthesia considerations for EVD management are multifaceted and require careful attention to:
- Pre-operative planning
- Induction technique
- Positioning and ventilation
- Intraoperative monitoring
- Medication management
- Emergence planning

By systematically addressing each of these areas, anesthesiologists can provide safe, evidence-based care for neurosurgical patients with EVDs, whether in the ICU or operating room setting.
